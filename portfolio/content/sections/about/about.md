I got into this work through networks and security, and that start still shapes how I build. I want to know where data goes, who can reach it, and what happens when something upstream fails. Over time my work moved up the stack, from infrastructure to the services and APIs that run on top of it, and that is where I've stayed.

Much of what I've built ends up in the hands of people doing hard jobs: the agent working a claim, the analyst onboarding a new customer, the partner who needs an answer in seconds instead of a phone call. I care about those people as much as the architecture behind them, and I've tried to keep both in view.

Lately I spend a lot of time on how engineering teams work with AI agents. I'm interested in making that work trustworthy: clear boundaries, deterministic checks, and a human signing off on the decisions that matter.

Through all of it, I want my work to help people, including communities that software often leaves out.
